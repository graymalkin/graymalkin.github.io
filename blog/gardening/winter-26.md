The start of autumn has been unseasonably warm this year, and after some much-needed rain the weeds are excitedly growing again at pace.
So, with some help from a friend, we pulled all the weeds in about a day from the cultivated part of our plot.
Excitingly, the weeds are beginning to change.
Where we've previously had endless bindweed we're now seeing Oxalis instead.
Oxalis is a bit like a giant shamrock, but underground it forms tight clumps of a dozen or so pale/white bulbs.
It's known as a pervasive weed on allotments, just as bindweed and Cooch grass, but it's far, far easier to deal with and less likely to strangle or dry out other plants.
To me: I prefer this weed than others!

We obviously do not want them just coming straight back though, so we're going to have a go at some no-dig method.
We scrounged around some local businesses for cardboard, and Iceland turned out to be the best.
A boot full of supermarket crisp boxes later and we're good to go.
We've covered the whole plot in cardboard and used a heavy layer of bark chippings around the blackberry bushes.
Once the [solar work](/blog/solar-install/) is fully done and our drive is back under control I'll order a pallet of well-rotted manure.

It's more expensive than buying it from a local farmer, but without a van or trailer, and with our allotment right down the far end of the field, we can't really get manure delivered in any other way.
We'll have a pallet of 60x50L bags popped on the end of our drive and ferry the remainder back/forth with my partner's Renault Clio.

I'm hoping it can be done in 5 trips, which will probably be a hard afternoon of work, but just one afternoon.

We're giving up on the fruit bushes, sadly, but I'm going to replace them with a row of slightly better positioned roses which I'll order bare-root in November.
I'll pick shrub roses which are good for cutting, and ones with a nice strong scent.
I think cut roses might be among the best !/$ things we could grow, with a bunch of flowers costing at least £5, often £10, a few roses will pay dividends.
More importantly though, I like roses.
